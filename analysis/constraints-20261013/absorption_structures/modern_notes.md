# Modern absolute-spectrum follow-up (13 October 2026)

The strongest new absolute-scale result is [McKay et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-026-02888-5), the published version of [arXiv:2509.11846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11846). Its GINAN radiometer measured **antenna temperature** over 60–350 MHz at the Murchison Observatory on 23 October 2024. It supplies a [machine-readable primary collection](https://doi.org/10.25919/8je1-fb10) containing calibrated temperature versus frequency/LST and frequency-dependent SKALA4.1 antenna patterns. DataCite reports 7.87 GB for the collection, so this bounded pass did not download it. The [authors' analysis repository](https://github.com/csiro/ginan-gsm) is public; its notebook and several small calibration files are separate from the large primary temperature HDF5 file. **Acquired primary McKay temperature samples: 0.**

McKay's published equations 1–2 are transcribed in `modern_sources.json`. They correct **GSM2016 specifically** by subtracting a frequency-dependent offset and then multiplying by a frequency-dependent scale. The correction is valid only at 60–350 MHz. The paper reports roughly 1.2 scale at 60–200 MHz and 1.5 by 350 MHz, with about 100 K offset subtraction below 100 MHz. Its 150-MHz measurement uncertainty is 1.22% rms; the corrected GSM2016 uncertainty is 1.81% rms. Those numbers are *fitted model corrections and summary uncertainties*, not independent rows of measured temperature. Comparing lowsky requires synthesizing antenna temperature with the released beam, LST sampling, ground/ionospheric terms, RFI mask, and matched sky coverage. There is **no valid direct 1–50 MHz application** or justified extrapolation of these polynomials.

For an in-band absolute anchor, [Monsalve et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/abd558) use EDGES total-power drift scans to fit the Guzmán 45-MHz map's scale **1.076 ± 0.034 (2σ)** and zero level **−160 ± 78 K (2σ)**. They also fit the 150-MHz Landecker–Wielebinski map: scale **1.112 ± 0.023** and zero level **0.7 ± 6.0 K**, both 2σ. These are corrections to existing maps, not stand-alone full-sky measurements. Whether the 45-MHz result is an independent lowsky validation depends on the model/input-map lineage; even if the map was not fitted directly, the calibration reference and map must be tracked separately.

The only small public spectrum acquired here is the [EDGES Bowman et al. 2018 Figure 1 CSV](https://loco.lab.asu.edu/edges/edges-data-release/), downloaded via [the release's direct CSV endpoint](https://loco.lab.asu.edu/download/790/) to `sources/edges_bowman2018_fig1.csv`. SHA-256: `6eebf9cd0a73280c002f630406e6692d7408e59f37bec1aef3664ceaac1cb030`. It has **128 frequency rows**, 50.012–99.621 MHz at 0.390625-MHz spacing, of which **123 have Weight=1** (first/last valid 51.184/98.840 MHz). Only `Tsky` is a reduced measured antenna-temperature spectrum; `Tres1`, `Tres2`, `Tmodel`, and `T21` depend on foreground/global-signal fitting. The source's band is entirely at or above 50 MHz. This spectrum is useful as a beam-averaged calibration check after a forward operator, while the claimed 21-cm profile should not be treated as a diffuse Galactic constraint.

The [LEDA 50–87-MHz analysis](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/505/2/1575/6276736) gives a northern-sky beam-averaged spectral index around −2.50 to −2.56 over LST 9–12.5 h, with 0.09–0.12 scatter/error in its combined estimate. It explicitly adjusts its **absolute** temperature scale to an extrapolation of the Guzmán 45-MHz map, so it is a slope comparison rather than a fresh zero-level anchor. The paper says underlying data are available on request; no contact was made. [SARAS 110–175 MHz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.7762) offers an older cross-instrument check but is outside lowsky's 1–50 MHz range; no per-channel data were acquired here.

[arXiv:2211.16547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16547) is **a workshop report**, despite containing a section about EDGES. It is neither a new EDGES spectrum nor an independent data release. The primary EDGES papers and [LoCo Lab releases](https://loco.lab.asu.edu/edges/edges-data-release/) are the appropriate sources.

Priority for the paper: first use the **45-MHz EDGES map scale/zero-level** as an in-band absolute-scale sensitivity panel after checking shared map lineage. Second, treat **McKay's 60–350-MHz spectrum** as an external high-frequency extension/normalization challenge and acquire a selected small temperature-plus-beam subset from the release if a 60–350-MHz comparison is pursued. Third, use the 123 valid reduced EDGES bins as a beam-averaged auxiliary spectrum. No modern source found in this pass directly anchors the most prior-sensitive 1–10-MHz sky.
