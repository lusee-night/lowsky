# Absorption and named-structure constraints (13 October 2026)

Modern absolute-spectrum follow-up and its acquired EDGES CSV are catalogued in [`modern_notes.md`](modern_notes.md) and [`modern_sources.json`](modern_sources.json).

The usable numerical set here is **152 H II sightlines at 76.2 MHz** from the [CDS electronic table for Su et al. 2018](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/MNRAS/479/4041), plus **four 22-MHz loop averages** from [Urosevic & Borka Jovanovic 2011, Tables 1–2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.3354). All 152 sightlines have two measured brightness temperatures, distances, optical depths, and front/behind emissivities; 28 optical depths are lower limits (`>`). The CDS archive has only the 76.2-MHz table despite the paper describing six-frequency fits. The named Gum and Orion values in `structure_constraints.json` are published *approximate* prose or fitted-prior quantities, not additional precise holdout table rows.

## Data and provenance

- `hii_absorption_76p2mhz.json`: complete 152-row transcription of the 179-character CDS fixed-width `tablea1.dat`, preserving errors, limit flags, names, and Galactic coordinates parsed from names. `build_records.py` is the reproducible converter. The [CDS ReadMe](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/ReadMe/J/MNRAS/479/4041?format=html) supplies every byte range and unit. `Th` and `Tm` are observed GLEAM temperatures, in **1000 K**; all `Tb`, `Tf`, `Xb`, `Xf`, `tau`, and emissivities are derived. The source footprint is -40° < l < 40°, -2° < b < 4°. The 76.2-MHz band is 7.68 MHz wide and has a 5.41′ × 4.43′ beam. The paper's equation 6 jointly fits six frequencies, so the 76.2-MHz fitted fields are correlated with other bands and should not be treated as six independent measurements.
- `structure_constraints.json`: four background-subtracted 22-MHz loop excesses with published ±500 K errors, spectral indices and contour bounds, plus explicitly labeled approximate/fitted Gum and Orion constraints. The 22-MHz [DRAO survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.3354) beam is 1.1° × 1.7°, with about 500 K effective sensitivity. The loop authors select pixels between `Tmin` and `Tmax`, which exclude diffuse background and confusing sources, and subtract a nearby off-loop background. Reproduce that aperture/background rule on simulations; point-sampling a bright rim is not equivalent.
- `sources/`: five downloaded original items with hashes below; the text extractions aid audit and are deterministic `pdftotext -layout` outputs. No figure values were digitized. Downloaded files total about 3.2 MB.

## Independence and interpretation

The [local conditioning record](../../../references/our_sky_conditioning.md) explicitly uses Purcell's Gum fit, Orion geometry, the historical Gum 4-MHz depression, and low-frequency loop work. Those are **prior checks**, not blind validation. The 152 GLEAM H II sightlines are **candidate holdouts**: measured on/off GLEAM columns are new to the stated direct lowsky conditioning, but the full ULSA/input lineage has not been audited. Su et al.'s fitted emissivities use Haslam 408-MHz total power extrapolated with brightness spectral index -2.7, a 20-kpc Galactic edge, H II distances from other catalogues, and a missing-short-spacing solution. The paper states changing the index to -2.6 lowers emissivities by about 15%, outside the tabulated one-sigma errors. It also omits diffuse WIM and unresolved H II absorption from that reconstruction. Use the **measured on/off temperatures** for the most direct radio check and the **derived emissivities** for a conditional physics comparison.

**Forward-operator gate:** 76.2 MHz is outside the repository's nominal 1–50 MHz illustrated/validated range, and most few-arcminute H II absorbers cannot be represented at the baseline NSIDE=32, roughly 2° pixels. A comparison must first establish a justified 76.2-MHz extrapolation and generate/smooth a high-resolution or explicit sub-grid H II absorber observation with the same GLEAM beam, spatial filtering, on/off apertures, and selection. Treat plots 1–2 as proposed diagnostics until that operator exists; direct NSIDE=32 pixel-to-catalogue residuals would be misleading.

[Alexander et al. 1971](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1971ApJ...167..487A) report that 3.93/4.7-MHz Gum-region brightness is approximately one-third to one-half of a 30–150-MHz extrapolation in 240° < l < 280°, -15° < b < 15°. Their `tau_4 < 3 ± 1` is an upper limit, and their EM = 1300 pc cm^-6 and high-temperature estimate belong to an older model; do not use those as modern EM truth. [Pon et al. 2014](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/441/2/1095/1066551) describe roughly 25 R Eridanus filaments and roughly 5 R adjacent wall with no numerical errors, and use 2.25 pc cm^-6 per R at assumed 8000 K. [Purcell et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.06296) derive n_e = 1.3 ± 0.4 cm^-3, outer radius 22.7 ± 0.1°, wall thickness 18.5 +1.5/-1.4 pc, and warm-gas **volume** filling factor 0.3 +0.3/-0.1 by fitting RM plus H-alpha in the relatively clean upper Gum region. These fit parameters are correlated and do not imply a degree-scale projected covering fraction. Extinction, electron temperature, and geometry affect any H-alpha-to-EM comparison.

## Four concrete paper plots

1. **H II absorption contrasts:** scatter `Th` against `Tm` in the 152 GLEAM rows, or plot `Tm - Th` versus longitude. Use the published 76.2-MHz beam/bandpass and local off-region definitions in mock observations. Show measured errors, and distinguish direct contrasts from fitted optical depths. This is the strongest independent radio panel.
2. **H II emissivity versus path:** at each sightline's (l, b, distance), compare simulated path-averaged foreground/background 76.2-MHz brightness with the table's `emf` and `emb`, with asymmetric errors and 28 lower-limit optical-depth markers where relevant. A 20-kpc edge, Haslam scaling, and missing flux make this conditional rather than a pure data residual.
3. **Loop excess comparison:** four-point 22-MHz panel of model minus observed spur excess with ±500 K error bars and the original contour/off-loop extraction. Overlay spectral beta only as a prior-sensitivity curve, since the repository already cites this paper. Flag Loop IV, for which the authors caution background selection may depress the 22-MHz value.
4. **Gum / Orion thermal-screen envelope:** show synthetic brightness-ratio spectra through the Gum aperture and H-alpha-derived EM ranges for Orion arcs/wall. Annotate the Gum 4-MHz ratio as an approximate band (1/3–1/2) and Orion 25 R/5 R as approximate reference levels without invented error bars. Both are conditioning checks; account for dust extinction, temperature, unresolved clumping, and beam averaging.

The 2017 [Su et al. 47-H II survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05446) overlaps the later 152-feature GLEAM work, so it is a candidate *cross-method* comparison, not 47 more independent lines of sight. Its preprint abstract and an ANU repository PDF surfaced different 88-MHz emissivity summary values; I did not mix either aggregate into these records without resolving the version/method difference. A distinct [Hindson et al. 2016 MWA H II catalogue](https://vizier.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/other/PASA/33.20) has 302 entries and could support a separate selection/flux study, but it has not been downloaded or counted as usable measurements here.
