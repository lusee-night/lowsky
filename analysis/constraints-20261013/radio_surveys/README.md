# Resolved radio-survey comparison inputs (2026-10-13)

`manifest.json` separates three **downloaded observed maps** (Caswell/DRAO 10.03 MHz, DRAO 22 MHz, Maipu/MU nominal 45 MHz) from two **catalogued but not downloaded** map families (LWA1 35–80 MHz and reprocessed Haslam 408 MHz). The downloaded files total 4,155,840 bytes. Their direct NASA LAMBDA URLs, SHA-256 values, HDU, HEALPix metadata, and measured valid-pixel counts are in the manifest. The 10/22/45 pixel values themselves are source observations; the counts are local FITS inspection, not fitted science statistics. Explicit redistribution permission was not found, so provenance is retained and licensing should be checked before publishing the FITS files elsewhere.

## Comparison plots to make

1. **10/22/45 map morphology:** Evaluate `generate_sky` at 10.03, 22, and 45 MHz. Convert model Galactic RING maps to the survey's Galactic NESTED grid, or both to a common grid. Form each common mask from finite survey pixels, the documented sentinels, beam-edge/coverage limits, and source apertures. Convolve the higher-resolution side to a common *effective* beam before regridding/downsampling. The historical beams are anisotropic and position dependent, so a conservative 5-degree Gaussian common beam is a practical low-ell first pass; flag the approximation. Plot survey/model difference and residual versus Galactic latitude/longitude, along with a robust slope from the intersecting valid region. Fit a separate additive zero-level nuisance and multiplicative calibration nuisance per survey, then also display the unadjusted result. Never use a full-sky mean for the partial-sky surveys.
2. **Absorption morphology and color:** Make matched-resolution 10/22 and 22/45 ratios or temperature spectral-index maps on the intersection masks. Use paired local annulus/background measurements around large H II depressions and Galactic-center plane sectors; preserve their source removal and masking. Roger et al.'s 22-MHz map resolves the North Polar Spur and an extended absorption band. The 45-MHz north is actually 46.5-MHz input; propagate that effective frequency in any color index rather than silently using 45 everywhere.
3. **LWA1 direct test:** Download a limited set such as 35, 45, 50 MHz plus paired uncertainty maps from the [LAMBDA listing](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/foreground/fg_lwa1_radio_maps_get.html). Rotate from its equatorial RING HEALPix grid to a common Galactic grid using pixel-window and beam-aware operations. LWA1's lower-temperature artifact near the southern coverage edge and other known calibration features warrant an equatorial-declination diagnostic and a conservative boundary exclusion. Treat each uncertainty map as a spatial scatter term and add the paper's 15–25% shared calibration systematic, not nine independent scale errors. Avoid comparing its 45-MHz map to the Maipu/MU map as nine independent frequencies; these are separate instruments but correlated by common sky and some total-power references.
4. **Haslam 408-MHz diffuse APS:** Download the [destriped, desourced measured map and bitmask](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/foreground/fg_2014_haslam_408_get.html), not the synthetic `ns2048` small-scale-added map. Construct matched bright-source masks, remove monopole/dipole on the retained region, account for masked-sky mode coupling, and compare beam- and pixel-window-corrected `C_ell` only over an ell range supported by both maps. This repository documents diffuse-morphology convergence only through ell=64, so ell≈20–64 is a natural first band. La Porta et al.'s APS slope of -3.0 to -2.6 is a useful qualitative range, but the actual fit must reproduce its sky mask and multipoles. Do not use full-sky pseudo-`C_ell` as though it were an unbiased masked-sky estimate.

For any diffuse-only comparison, remove or mask the modeled analytic A-team sources consistently with the survey. The 22-MHz map already removes Cas A, Cyg A, Tau A, and Vir A; the reprocessed Haslam map removes many extragalactic sources while retaining bright Galactic ones. The LWA1 published images include compact sources. Comparisons of residual maps and APS will otherwise measure source-policy differences.

## Independence and calibration limits

The repository [README](../../../README.md) and [`pipeline.py`](../../../src/lowsky/pipeline.py) show global tuning to mounted ULSA at 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 MHz. [`our_sky_conditioning.md`](../../../references/our_sky_conditioning.md) and [`local_features_literature.md`](../../../references/local_features_literature.md) document 408-MHz loop contrasts, low-frequency loop spectra, and measured sky geography used as priors. ULSA and other model products also ingest empirical low-frequency maps (see [LAMBDA model-dependency table](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/foreground/fg_templates.html)). Therefore an observed 10/22/45/LWA comparison tests morphology and regional residuals most clearly, but its absolute spectral amplitude is conditioned on overlapping survey information. The DRAO 22-MHz temperature scale itself was adjusted by declination using Haslam 408 MHz ([Roger et al. 1999](https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9902213)). Explicitly label those shared anchors in the paper's plot caption.

The strongest ready measurements here are the actual 10/22/45 survey pixels and published, source-attributed summary values in `manifest.json`. LWA1 and Haslam currently have verified direct download URLs and survey metadata only; there is no local map, extracted pixel statistic, cross-spectrum, or fitted comparison for them yet. No numerical model-vs-observation residual is claimed here. For the 45-MHz absolute scale, the [LAMBDA product note](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/foreground/fg_maipu_info.html) reports that the [EDGES correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11019) has **not** been applied to its hosted map.
