# Modern low-frequency evidence for `lowsky`

The ranked source metadata and exact public links are in [`modern_sources.json`](modern_sources.json). The only new local modern product is the official [LoDeSS DR1 source catalogue](data/LoDeSS_MFS-I-image-pb.pybdsf.srl.fits): 7,545,600 bytes, SHA-256 `de267b464ec63c5e031e63cae61f231650419c12cf74632cf15e69d9791151ed`, with 21,465 rows and 45 columns in FITS extension 1. This is measured catalogue content, not measured diffuse brightness. The [official release page](https://lofar-surveys.org/lodess.html) and [Cordun et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04698) establish the October 2026 first release, 40 fields at 15–30 MHz, and strong field-dependent ionospheric flux uncertainty. The paper recommends adding 39% systematic uncertainty for bright, isolated compact-source flux comparisons.

For the present 1–50-MHz model, prioritize [LWA1/LEDA 35–50-MHz maps](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/foreground/fg_lwa1_radio_maps_get.html) as the modern degree-scale diffuse constraint: their processing restores missing large scales using LEDA total power, though their absolute calibration is still at the 15–25% level. [OVRO-LWA's 36.528 and 46.992-MHz maps](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/foreground/fg_ovrolwa_radio_maps_get.html) offer sharper structure over much of the same northern sky; [Eastwood et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00466) explicitly state that the interferometer-only maps lack a global sky brightness level. Compare OVRO-LWA only after matching its spatial transfer response or subtracting common large-scale modes. Its direct HEALPix files are about 403 MB each, so none was downloaded here.

[LoLSS DR1](https://lofar-surveys.org/lolss.html) has 54-MHz field mosaics, including a source-subtracted low-resolution product, but its current released HETDEX area is a compact/small-scale check, not a new absolute diffuse map. [LoDeSS DR1](https://lofar-surveys.org/lodess.html) now adds the most useful modern 23-MHz compact-source check. Neither its 45-arcsec images nor its catalogue can be compared directly to `lowsky`'s degree-scale diffuse temperature or used to fix the monopole. Use them for A-team/bright-source policy, source-count context, and targeted resolved features after the same angular and spatial filtering. The catalogue excludes fields labelled poor, while the release still makes their images available.

The June 2026 [GINAN/SKALA absolute radiometry study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02888-5) measures beam-averaged brightness at 60–350 MHz. Its [machine-data DOI](https://doi.org/10.25919/8je1-fb10) is identified in the paper; the archive did not permit direct file verification here. This is useful for a future model extension beyond 50 MHz only after convolution with its antenna pattern and the observed sidereal-time sampling. Its quoted precision and GSM2016 correction must not be attached to the current 1–50-MHz pixel predictions.

These modern surveys do not replace the historical [Caswell 10-MHz](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/foreground/fg_drao_10_info.html) and [DRAO 22-MHz](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/foreground/fg_drao_info.html) total-brightness maps for diffuse absorption comparisons below 30 MHz. LoDeSS provides a powerful independent source check in that band, while the older total-power surveys remain the resolved diffuse checks. No map-vs-model statistic was computed in this pass.
