# Thermal structures: object-specific observational constraints

As of 2026-10-13. `measurements.json` contains **50 traceable records**: 23 Gum, 15 Orion–Eridanus, 12 Cygnus X. A record is a published line measurement, Hα pointing, fitted parameter, filament summary, or an image product; these are **not 49 independent likelihood data points**. `source_manifest.json` gives the URLs and SHA-256 for six modest downloaded primary products (including a 2.5 MB FITS map). PDF-extracted `.txt` files are convenience copies of the downloaded PDFs. The papers and their numerical tables, rather than those OCR copies, are authoritative.

## Current lowsky comparison

[`src/lowsky/pipeline.py`](../../../src/lowsky/pipeline.py) uses a Gum radial screen nominally at 450 pc with an effective Gaussian EM and a heuristic *beam areal covering* `1-exp(-EM/70)`. The saved [`feature_fidelity_validation.json`](../../../outputs_our_sky_gum_gaussian_final/feature_fidelity_validation.json) reports Gum effective EM max 106.2 pc cm⁻⁶, median 39.4 in its regional anchor, and mean effective areal covering 0.631; the pipeline's center coefficient 190 is **not** the final saved maximum after the ray/beam operator. Orion uses two partial screens centered at 250 and 330 pc; its saved regional effective EM max is 25.8 pc cm⁻⁶ and mean screen areal covering 0.0351. Cygnus X is a diffuse 3D Gaussian centered 1.45 kpc with saved regional EM max 2118.6 pc cm⁻⁶. The validation status is `pass_with_scope_limit`, through ell=64. At NSIDE=32, angular cells are ~1.8° across, far broader than a 2′ LOFAR beam or the 22–33′ Gum RRL pointings.

**Gum.** [Woermann et al. (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-8711.2000.03344.x) measured H recombination line peaks of 4.7–6.7 mK in five frequency/pointing combinations at 1715.670 and 2422.463 MHz. Their line/thermal-continuum analysis gave Te 4200±1400 to 8500±2400 K and EM 220±60 to 470±130 pc cm⁻⁶ at selected 22–33′ beams. These are **selected bright continuum positions**; forcing the entire broad Gum screen to their EM would be unjustified. They are, however, evidence of sub-beam or regional high-EM material beyond the saved broad-screen 106 maximum. The [2026 Kumar–Deshpande–Jain pulsar study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05791) infers a Vela-foreground scatterer at 254±16 pc and fits a substantially different ellipsoidal electron-density component (center 341 pc, scale 78.3 pc, width 32.6 pc). This is a DM/scattering model fit with unreported per-parameter errors, not a direct free–free wall distance. Its center (264.2°, −2.63°) also differs from the pipeline Gum center (258°, −6.6°). The older [Purcell et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.06296) RM/Hα shell and warm-gas volume filling factor are already invoked in the repo's conditioning notes; the `f≈0.3` **volume** fraction does not determine the model's *projected areal* covering. A newer [IRAS Vela shell 3D dust study](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/origin-of-the-iras-vela-shell-new-insights-from-3d-dust-mapping/) is contextual geometry only until the dust-to-ionized boundary offset is measured; dust cannot be treated as an ionized-screen edge.

**Orion–Eridanus.** [Madsen, Reynolds & Haffner (2006), Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0609558) supplies fifteen measured 1° WHAM pointings, including an A–G cut through a southern Hα filament: 1.0±0.1, 6.6±0.1, 14.9±0.1, 13.7±0.1, 7.3±0.1, 3.5±0.1, 1.3±0.1 R. All are ON minus nearby OFF (~0.5 R); quoted errors are statistical, while the North America Nebula intensity calibration carries ~10% common error. No extinction correction was applied (authors quote regional A_V~0.15 mag); converting Hα to EM requires Te and extinction and yields approximately 2.25 pc cm⁻⁶ per Rayleigh at 8000 K before dust. The full Table 3 spans 1–364 R, with bright stellar regions and bubble positions deliberately selected, so a uniform-screen comparison is invalid. [Joubaud et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10083) place **H I/CO** shell components roughly 150–250 pc away, while the model screens sit at 250 and 330 pc; neutral/molecular distances alone do not fix the ionized wall. [Foley et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01405) reconstruct Orion dust geometry, again not an Hα/free–free boundary. The repo already cites Orion Hα and shell morphology, so WHAM sightlines are at best a *conditional/partial holdout* until the exact conditioning pixel provenance is audited.

**Cygnus X.** [Emig et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.09193) provide a public [CDS short-spacing-corrected LOFAR image](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/ReadMe/J/A+A/664/A88?format=html&tex=true), 801×801 pixels, 148.046 MHz, Jy/beam, 2′ beam; the map and its catalog metadata are saved here. In the 4°×4° field the paper finds EM 10³–10⁶ pc cm⁻⁶, characteristic filament width 4.3 pc, median fitted peak EM 5200 pc cm⁻⁶, and completeness near 4200 pc cm⁻⁶. Their Table 1 five selected filaments have peak EM 4200–7800 pc cm⁻⁶ and widths 3.6–5.3 pc. Te=7400 K and thermal decomposition enter the EM conversion; `n_e=sqrt(EM/width)` further assumes projected width equals LOS depth and may overstate density for sheets. The saved lowsky max 2119 pc cm⁻⁶ can represent only diffuse low-end structure; a **subgrid screen/mixture** is needed to predict those filaments at the coarse beam. The paper's abstract peak-EM slope −1.8±0.1 differs from its main-text fitted slope −2.0±0.2; the JSON retains the abstract value and no slope likelihood should be installed before reconciling selection/fit definitions. [Roshi et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/adfdd9) add GBT RRL mapping at 342, 800, and 5800 MHz, with masked diffuse-gas He⁺/H⁺ 0.06±0.02 and evidence for multiple velocity components; these are useful thermal/kinematic checks but not direct 1–50 MHz absorption. The paper's full table/product release could not be retrieved from the publisher in this bounded pass. [Rygl et al. parallaxes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.7023) support the complex's ∼1.4 kpc association while allowing depth and unrelated background sources; a single Gaussian distance is a simplification.

## Suggested paper plots and likelihood operators

1. **Gum selected-line comparison:** locate the five RRL sightlines, forward-convolve effective EM and thermal continuum to their 22–33′ beams, and plot observed line peak and EM-derived estimates with Te nuisance/error covariance. Condition the likelihood on the 2.3-GHz-bright selection; a coarse NSIDE=32 map requires a subgrid EM distribution, not direct point-to-pixel equality.
2. **Orion filament traverse:** plot the seven WHAM A–G Hα measurements against longitude/latitude arc length, with the model's 1° aperture and ON–OFF differencing applied. Fit common 10% calibration plus extinction and Te nuisance parameters. The OFF and WHAM survey map may share data with the model's existing Hα conditioning, so do not double-count without lineage audit.
3. **Cygnus thermal map:** convert the saved LOFAR FITS Jy/beam map with its 2′ beam and thermal/nonthermal model to a beam-matched 148-MHz map; compare smoothed 1–2° diffuse profiles and separate a 2′ filament PDF/cross-section panel. Include short-spacing/CGPS absolute-scale uncertainty and a nonthermal background. 148 MHz lies **outside the nominal 1–50 MHz validation range**; use it as a thermal-structure crosscheck, not a low-frequency spectral anchor.
4. **Distance hypotheses:** compare Gum 450-pc screen against 2026 pulsar-model 341-pc center and scatterer 254±16 pc, and Orion 250/330-pc screen versus neutral-shell 150–250 pc. Label the traced phase and model dependence on the graphic; do not fit one distance to every tracer.

There is no newly verified, public object-specific **1–50 MHz free–free absorption spectrum/contrast table** for all three objects in this pass. In particular, the 148-MHz Cygnus map and GHz RRL/Hα data constrain thermal structure through a forward operator but cannot by themselves validate 1–50 MHz absorption, spin temperature, or synchrotron foreground/background placement. The RRL and WHAM values are not independent of other radio/Hα maps simply because they appear in separate papers. The Gum RRL, Cygnus LOFAR, and modern pulsar geometry are candidate holdouts pending an input-lineage audit of lowsky and its inherited Haslam/CGPS/WHAM priors.
