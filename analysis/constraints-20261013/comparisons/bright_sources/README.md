# Bright-source comparison figures

Run `.venv/bin/python examples/compare_bright_sources.py --output-dir analysis/constraints-20261013/comparisons/bright_sources` from the repository root. The default input is the saved `outputs_our_sky_gum_gaussian_final/ateam_components.npz` (September 2 diagnostic output). `comparison_values.json` records the input paths and SHA-256 hashes, source-only fluxes converted from Kelvin maps to Jy, observed values, plotted predictions and unstandardized residuals. Its run manifest documents the settings but does not establish that the saved output was generated by the current source revision. No model generation or fitting is done here.

**`lwa1_source_ratios.{png,pdf}` caption.** [Dowell et al. (2017) Table 2](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/469/4/4537/3815529) measured Cas A, Tau A and Vir A flux ratios to Cyg A from 35–80 MHz; bars are the paper's reported `±` values. Twelve measurements at actual band centers below 50 MHz are strictly inside the saved 1–50 MHz model. Three nominal 50-MHz records have actual center 50.005 MHz and are compared at nominal 50 MHz; JSON flags them as approximations outside the strict range. Twelve higher-frequency measurements are context only; no model extrapolation is drawn or serialized there. Dark lines integrate lowsky's source-only maps, then divide by its Cyg A flux. This is an approximate measurement operator: LWA1 subtracted a local background annulus and fitted a Gaussian after its own beam/uv response. Its absolute scale rests on Cyg A, and ratio errors across frequencies can share calibration and background systematics. The figure establishes a visual comparison, not a matched-response goodness of fit.

**`cas_a_turnover.{png,pdf}` caption.** Saved lowsky Cas A *total-source* flux versus fitted peak summaries from [2019 GURT](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06104) and [2023.5 NenuFAR/URAN-2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09764). GURT's peak is 32.19 ± 1.894 kJy at 22.345 ± 0.5 MHz (uncertainty quoted in the same team's later paper); the 2023.5 peak is 29.88 ± 2.98 kJy at 21.5 ± 0.2 MHz. The two symbols describe fitted spectral maxima, not sampled spectrum points. Lowsky predicts 48.72 and 49.90 kJy at those respective frequencies, excesses of 16.53 and 20.02 kJy. Dividing these by the quoted errors gives 8.73 and 6.72, but these are **not formal significances** because the epochs, correlated Cyg A calibration, fitted-peak covariance and interferometric response are unmodeled. The current source component has no epoch/fading parameter; a physical follow-up should examine its 50-MHz normalization and front/back versus full-source absorption before fitting.

The 2025 Crab peak in `objects_bright_sources/measurements.json` belongs to the extended **shell**, whereas lowsky's Tau A component is total-source. It is omitted from quantitative overlays. Cyg A's resolved hotspot indices are also component, rather than whole-source, observables.

Validation: `tests/test_bright_source_comparison.py` checks the full-sky Kelvin-solid-angle to Jy conversion against known fluxes and ensures frequencies beyond 50 MHz produce no prediction except the documented 50.005-MHz nominal-band approximation. Both tests pass.
