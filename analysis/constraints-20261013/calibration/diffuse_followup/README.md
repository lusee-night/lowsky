# Diffuse regional discrepancy: bounded root-cause audit

This is a read-only diagnosis of the saved model and acquired maps, not a replacement fit. The matched-map operator and input hashes are in [`../radio_regions`](../../comparisons/radio_regions/README.md); exploratory low-dimensional fits are explicitly rejected in [`../diffuse`](../diffuse/README.md).

In the common southern Orion–Eridanus footprint, the 22-MHz survey bin mean is **20.23 kK**, while the saved source-free lowsky components sum to **44.12 kK**: 22.60 kK extragalactic, 17.81 kK volume synchrotron, 3.68 kK local shells, and 0.017 kK free-free. The extragalactic component alone exceeds the survey mean. At 45 MHz the survey mean is **4.45 kK**, versus model **6.87 kK** (3.58 extragalactic, 2.76 volume synchrotron, 0.52 shells, 0.004 free-free). These are arithmetic means of matched, band-limited, 12°-smoothed longitude bins; survey calibration/zero-level errors have not been fitted. The source survey FITS grids and units agree with the manifest (Galactic NESTED, 10/22 K; 45 metadata lacks TUNIT but LAMBDA describes brightness K). No RING/NESTED or K-unit error was found.

The model's extragalactic term is hardcoded in [`model.py`](../../../../src/lowsky/model.py) as `1.2*(ν/GHz)^−2.58 K`, yielding **22.65 kK at 22 MHz** before negligible circumgalactic attenuation. This is a substantive model assumption, not a discovered software bug. [Roger et al. (1999)](https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9902213) discuss a historical source-count estimate of about **6 kK** at 22 MHz, note that the extrapolation from >150 MHz is uncertain, allow a possible **5 kK** 22-MHz zero-level error, and report T–T offsets around ±10 kK. They also adjusted the declination-dependent 22-MHz gain using Haslam 408 MHz. Lowsky's `tune_to_ulsa` objective matches *full-sky means* of the volume/background baseline at nine frequencies, omitting the local shells; it does not fit these regional survey pixels and ULSA has overlapping empirical low-frequency ancestry. Thus the disparity cannot be assigned uniquely to either EGB normalization or the 22-MHz map zero level.

Adding an Orion thermal screen alone cannot reconcile both bands. At Te=8000 K a foreground slab would need τ22≈**1.083**, or EM≈**807 pc cm⁻⁶**, to transform the 44.12-kK model mean into the 20.23-kK survey mean if it covered *all* modeled emission. The saved Orion effective EM max is 25.8 pc cm⁻⁶ (τ22≈0.035). More decisively, the 45-MHz model mean **6.87 kK is already below Te=8 kK**; any additional isothermal thermal screen would raise its brightness, while the observed mean is lower at 4.45 kK. This does not rule out complex absorption with different temperatures, depth placement or calibration, but it rules out the simple opacity-only fix.

A defensible next implementation needs an external absolute low-frequency background constraint and survey-specific offset/gain nuisance terms, then a **joint** fit of volume synchrotron, background, and ionized structure using the same forward operator and a regional holdout. Until that is available, leave the production model and conditioned loop amplitudes unchanged. A global two-parameter scale that suppresses loops is not a physical solution to this regional gap.
