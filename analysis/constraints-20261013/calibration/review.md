# Bounded calibration review — 2026-10-13

This is an audit of the existing API and proposed narrow calibration, not a new observational fit. Sources are the repo's `src/lowsky/model.py`, `src/lowsky/pipeline.py`, and the dated evidence bundles. No model parameters were changed here.

## Diffuse update: component-consistent route

`SkyParameters.emissivity_scale` multiplies only the volume synchrotron emissivity in `transfer_frequency`. The returned total also contains local radio shells, free-free emission, and an extragalactic term. `emission_measure_scale` changes attenuation and free-free emission; `spectral_index_offset` changes the volume synchrotron exponent. Therefore **a best-fit multiplier of the whole total map is not a valid `emissivity_scale` preset**. Applying a factor to a precomputed total map would also scale thermal and local components and break the model API. The present generation path calls `tune_to_ulsa` unconditionally, which can erase an externally fitted diffuse preset unless bypassed explicitly.

For a small, scientifically coherent calibration, hold the fixed geometry, EM fields, local shells, and extragalactic term constant, and fit only `emissivity_scale` plus a common `spectral_index_offset` to held-in survey statistics using the model's transfer function. If performance matters, for fixed EM and all other parameters, a cached volume-synchrotron component can be reweighted exactly by `(new_emissivity_scale / baseline_emissivity_scale) * (nu/408 MHz) ** (new_beta_offset - baseline_beta_offset)` before adding *unchanged* shell/free-free/extragalactic components. This identity is conditional on the fixed EM/curvature fields and should be tested against a direct `transfer_frequency` evaluation at several frequencies. Do not reinterpret this as a global antenna-temperature gain.

Compare with observed 10, 22 and/or 45 MHz maps only after matching beam, available sky mask, bright-source treatment, and regional statistic. The 22-MHz declination gain was adjusted using Haslam 408 MHz; its zero level and map coverage are nontrivial. The 10-MHz survey has limited coverage and an unverified absolute zero level; 45 MHz has its own map-scale uncertainty. Thus a conditional regional *shape/scale* calibration may be feasible, but one should not simply regress full-sky mean model temperatures against partial-sky map means or treat per-pixel errors as independent. Reserve at least one survey/band/region untouched by the fit for a check. Any fit-point plot must be labeled **training agreement**, and a calibration inherited from ULSA-related surveys is not a blind independent validation of the prior family. The baseline `tune_to_ulsa` already used ULSA all-sky means and should be disabled for an external-survey preset, while kept for the historical preset.

## Cas A turnover: conditional two-parameter update

`ateam_integrated_k_sr` currently uses `S50=27,104 Jy`, `alpha=-0.77`, `tau10=0.35`, source distance 3.4 kpc, and modeled foreground free-free optical depth. The intrinsic factor is `exp[-tau10 * ((nu/10)^-2.1 - (50/10)^-2.1)]` below 50 MHz, so it is unity at 50 MHz by construction. `S50` is an **imposed 2020 LOFAR/external-spectrum calibration anchor**, not an independent integrated-flux observation. A two-parameter `(S50,tau10)` fit is meaningful only conditional on fixed alpha, MW foreground EM/temperature, instrument extraction, and epoch. The chosen source-only spectrum should be compared to source-only flux/ratios, not peak pixel temperature of a beam-smoothed map.

The modern 2019 and 2023.5 fitted Cas A peaks are epoch-dependent summaries: respectively about 32,190±1,894 Jy at 22.345±0.5 MHz and 29,880±2,980 Jy at 21.5±0.2 MHz. The 2023.5 peak and 38-MHz ratio are inferred from overlapping data, so they are correlated. LWA1 Cas/Cyg ratios share the Cyg A denominator and calibration. Avoid independent Gaussian multiplication of peak, ratio and LOFAR-imposed S50 constraints; do not treat an S50 match as validation. If a minimal fit uses one epoch's peak and one contemporaneous ratio, label the result a conditional phenomenological tuning, report the exact inputs and fixed nuisance assumptions, and compare against a genuinely unused band/epoch with secular evolution stated. A nonnegative `tau10` and positive `S50` are sensible parameter bounds. Foreground attenuation may already be embedded in the empirical S50 anchor, so double-counting remains an audit question rather than an established error.

## Review of the first worker artifacts

The draft `src/lowsky/calibration.py` uses the component-consistent identity above and correctly does not multiply the free-free, extragalactic, or local-shell terms. The saved diffuse `fit.json` uses alternate longitude bins from the **same** 10.03/22/45-MHz surveys as its holdout. Its median absolute log residual improves on that internal split, but both fitted changes are at their imposed bounds: emissivity ratio ≈0.30000000035 and beta-offset delta ≈+0.24999999975. These values are a **boundary solution**, not a resolved interior parameter estimate; widening bounds and assessing input zero levels/extra/shell budgets could change them. One 10-MHz anticenter example has an extragalactic model term around 170.7 kK plus local shell tens of kK against observed total ≈206–254 kK. This leaves little room for volume synchrotron and plausibly drives the fit to its minimum amplitude. The artifact's listed train/held-out improvements are descriptive, not independent-survey validation or evidence that the physical emissivity prior should be revised globally. A deployed preset should retain this status and require a separate cross-survey/region check before claiming physics inference.

The initial diffuse script took the median of **each component separately** within each longitude bin, then added them, while the observed statistic was the median of survey total intensity. Since `median(S)+median(L)+...` generally differs from `median(S+L+...)`, this did not match the measurement operator. The worker corrected the script and regenerated `fit.json` using **means of observed and component pixels**, for which the component sum is exact. The revised fit still saturates both bounds and is marked `rejected_bound_saturated_diagnostic_only` / `do_not_integrate`. The retained before/after figure is a diagnostic of the constrained trial, not a deployed calibration.

The Cas `fit.json` derives S50≈21,166 Jy and tau10≈1.808 from the **same 2023.5 fitted peak's** location and amplitude; those are correlated summaries rather than two independent detections. The file correctly labels the result conditional, but its inferred foreground attenuation comes from a saved harmonic artifact whose source revision is marked unverified. Check the source formula/epoch provenance before using it to characterize physical Cas absorption. Its new S50 also conflicts with the earlier imposed 27,104-Jy calibration anchor by construction; neither value is a direct LOFAR holdout.
