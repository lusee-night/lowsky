# Parker Solar Probe/FIELDS: bounded source assessment (13 October 2026)

## Usable numeric product

The [Page et al. (2025) Radio Science paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024RS008137) explicitly links a [Zenodo table, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.13899786](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.13899786), of fitted Galactic-coordinate complex spherical-harmonic coefficients of sky brightness, (b_{2,0}), Re/Im (b_{2,1}), and Re/Im (b_{2,2}), with standard errors. We downloaded its entire 9.5 kB `PSP_FIELDS_RFS_b2m.txt` to `sources/`. `modern_sources.json` faithfully parses its **56 rows from 0.514 to 6.78 MHz**, preserving the file's numeric units header verbatim (`10-21 W m-2 sr-(1/2) Hz-1`). The source file has 72 rows from 0.209–6.78 MHz; its 16 rows below 0.5 MHz remain in the original file but are excluded from the JSON. The paper says data below 0.5 MHz fit poorly from quasi-thermal plasma noise. It also says the selected 0.5–6.8 MHz range contains 54 logarithmically spaced bins, whereas the linked file has 56 rows in that range. We retained all 56 rows and flag this unresolved count difference.

These are **inferred sky anisotropy coefficients derived from observed voltage power/cross spectra**, not direct per-direction temperatures, an all-sky mean, or a (b_{0,0}) monopole. The 2025 analysis fits antenna electrical axes jointly with the sky coefficients, removes a 24-minute rolling mean to isolate roll modulation, uses only cross spectra below 1.8 MHz due to quasi-thermal noise, and excludes solar/Jovian bursts and enhanced-noise intervals. Its table includes standard errors plus a 6% fractional electrical-length calibration term in quadrature. The linked file does not provide inter-coefficient/frequency covariance, so avoid treating rows as independent likelihood terms. A lowsky comparison should calculate coefficients of its full-sky **specific-intensity** maps in the same Galactic spherical-harmonic convention, then compare these coefficient spectra, perhaps with a separate uncertainty/calibration treatment. Do not overlay these values on a scalar brightness-temperature spectrum.

The 2025 paper calls the coefficients a new measurement relative to [Page et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202244621), which used linear least squares on PSP data and reported both (l=0) and (l=2) coefficients at 56 frequencies between 0.5 and 7 MHz. The 2025 work reuses the PSP roll data while fitting antenna orientation more fully, so the two Page papers are **correlated analyses**, not independent data sets. Page (2025) explicitly states that absolute antenna-length calibration requires comparison with older spacecraft sky measurements; it cites Page (2022) for that step. Thus its absolute coefficient amplitudes inherit a historical spacecraft calibration dependency, though the roll-dependent **shape/anisotropy** is measured by PSP. The Zenodo record's “related work” field points to the 2022 DOI even though the 2025 paper identifies the file as its own updated coefficient table; preserve both provenance links.

## Bassett et al. (2023) and absolute spectrum availability

[Bassett et al. (2023), ApJ 945, 134; arXiv:2301.09612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09612) analyzes five PSP coning rolls as roll-averaged and phase-binned FIELDS spectra. The original preprint scan is retained at `sources/bassett2023.pdf`. Its **measured** roll-averaged receiver spectra and model reconstructions are shown in Figure 6, without a printed machine-readable table found in this first pass. Table 5 contains fitted physical-model parameters, **not observed sky spectral samples**; none were copied into the measurement JSON. The roll-averaged model fits, but phase-binned measurements expose inadequacy of its smooth symmetric sky model. Its roll average is antenna weighted and differs from a full-sky mean.

The Bassett absolute receiver-to-brightness conversion assumes a V1–V2 effective length of 3.3 m chosen in Page (2022) for agreement with Novaco & Brown (1978), so its absolute scale is **not independent of RAE-2**. Bassett's forward model also adopts ULSA's emissivity form (paper §3.1), making its fitted model predictions unsuitable as a holdout test of the lowsky/ULSA prior family. PSP's measured phase modulation is still useful for evaluating lowsky morphology after applying the actual FIELDS beam and roll geometry. The publicly cited [PSP/FIELDS L2 HFR](https://doi.org/10.48322/GF9T-4082) and [LFR](https://doi.org/10.48322/RE4H-TK40) data are the route to rederive spectra, but that requires time-window selection, noise subtraction, antenna response, and spacecraft attitude; no bulk CDF files were acquired here.

## Plot recommendation and gaps

Use `modern_sources.json` for a dedicated (l=2) coefficient panel beside the historical absolute-spectrum panel, with coefficients labeled as **Page (2025) PSP-derived fits**. A compatible lowsky curve requires converting brightness temperature to specific intensity, matching Galactic harmonic normalization/phase and frequency, and carrying the PSP absolute-scale tie to RAE. Page (2022) (l=0) numerical coefficients and Bassett (2023) roll-averaged numeric spectra were not located as small verified tables in this pass. No values were digitized from their figures. The raw PSP CDF archive is available but beyond this bounded acquisition.
