# Absolute low-frequency spectrum constraints (13 October 2026)

`measurements.json` contains 15 **observational table entries**, not points digitized from graphs or predictions from a spectral fit. Its two data groups are eight new northern/southern high-latitude dipole results in [Cane (1979), Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/189.3.465) and seven selected RAE-2 north polar entries that Cane republishes in Table 2 under observer 15 = [Novaco & Brown (1978)](https://doi.org/10.1086/156009). The latter values are *the same RAE-2 observation*, not a separate Cane experiment. The source scans are held in `sources/` (ADS PDF editions); their OCR `.txt` copies are only a search aid. I checked the numbers against the rendered pages, because OCR drops exponents.

## Meaning of the columns

- `frequency_mhz`, `temperature_k` or `equivalent_aerial_temperature_k`, and `specific_intensity_w_m2_hz_sr` are the authors' rounded, printed values. The RAE frequencies in Cane's compilation are rounded (e.g. 3.9 versus 3.93 MHz in Novaco & Brown), so don't treat them as exact channel centers. The RAE 2.2 MHz line is the *first* of two observer-15 entries in Cane Table 2. No interpolation or Rayleigh–Jeans conversion was used to create the recorded values.
- `fractional_uncertainty` for Cane is her **estimated maximum accuracy/error**, not a statistical 1-sigma interval: north 13% at 5.2 MHz and 7% elsewhere; south 25% at 5.2 MHz and 15% elsewhere (section 4.2). For the RAE subset it is the approximate **total channel error** in [Novaco & Brown Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1086/156009): the paper combines relative error with an absolute term tied to IMP-6. These fractions are for the RAE channel, not a new fit to the rounded Cane table entries. Correlations and beam errors are not supplied as a covariance matrix. Don't use these as independent Gaussian weights.
- The brightness in W m^-2 Hz^-1 sr^-1 is spectral *specific intensity*. The RAE table's temperatures are **equivalent aerial temperatures**, with frequency-dependent, very broad antenna responses. Cane Table 1 labels its own values brightness temperature. The brightness and temperature columns are printed at low precision and need not agree exactly after unit conversion.

## Sky weighting and comparison rule

Cane used scaled half-wave east–west dipoles above wire ground screens at Llanherne (Tasmania) and Penticton (Canada), looking toward declinations about -43 and +49 degrees. The values represent broad high-latitude fields near the Galactic poles; they are **not a 4-pi sky mean**. The 5.2 MHz south result rests on one observation. The paper discusses ionospheric absorption, interference, mismatch, and noise-source calibration (sections 2–4). Its Table 1 values can be compared to a lowsky *beam and pointing weighted* high-latitude prediction, with a broader region/beam envelope until the exact historical response is implemented. Plot north and south separately.

RAE-2 used a 229 m travelling-wave V antenna in lunar orbit. Its beam varies strongly with frequency: Novaco & Brown Table 1 lists half-power widths from about 39° × 61° at 9.18 MHz to roughly 300° × 170° at 0.25 MHz. The experiment filtered solar/Earth contamination and binned measurements into 5° Galactic cells, then smoothed the maps to antenna resolution. A RAE "north polar" spectrum is therefore **frequency-dependent beam-weighted antenna temperature**, particularly at 1 MHz and below, where it cannot be interpreted as an isolated sky pixel. Use a convolved model if the historical beam is available; otherwise display these as broad-region contextual points with an explicit beam mismatch band. RAE's absolute error budget incorporates an IMP-6 calibration term, so RAE and IMP-6 should not be treated as fully independent absolute scales.

The repository [README](../../../README.md) says lowsky is tuned to ULSA's global calibration, and [`tune_to_ulsa`](../../../src/lowsky/pipeline.py) fits global mean spectral parameters to ULSA. A lowsky–ULSA mean overlay is a calibration reproduction, not an independent test. These Cane and RAE points are observational inputs with potentially shared historical ancestry to ULSA. Independence requires checking ULSA's actual literature/calibration inputs before presenting a holdout claim. An **all-sky model mean cannot be compared point-for-point to these high-latitude / broad-beam spectra**; it can only be shown as clearly labeled context, while a proper follow-up must calculate the matching sky weighting.

## Other primary sources examined; gaps

- [Brown (1973), IMP-6](https://doi.org/10.1086/151968) measured 22 frequencies from 130 to 2600 kHz and reports minimum/estimated maximum dipole spectra. Its Table 1 is a rotated scanned figure/table with difficult OCR, and those spectra are not an all-sky mean. The original scan is retained, but no Brown points have been transcribed here. A manually verified extraction and calibration-covariance treatment would be needed before inclusion.
- [Manning & Dulk (2001), Wind/WAVES](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361:20010516) measured spin modulation and phase over 0.2–13.8 MHz. Its key quantitative result is a 3.6 MHz modulation minimum, with higher-frequency maximum toward the Galactic center and lower-frequency maximum nearer the Galactic poles. The paper's brightness-temperature panel compiles previous spectra, while the Wind measurements shown there are modulation/phase. Thus this paper is a useful **morphology/turnover-location check**, not a source of independent absolute brightness points in this file.
- [Dulk et al. (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361:20000006) combines Wind/WAVES and Bruny Island observations with a Galactic-background calibration model. That empirical curve reuses earlier sky spectra. It should be plotted as a *fit/reference model*, not as independent measured points. I found no verified machine-readable absolute table for this selection pass.
- [Voyager PRA instrument paper](https://pds-ppi.igpp.ucla.edu/data/VG1-S-PRA-3-RDR-LOWBAND-6SEC-V1.0/DOCUMENT/INSTRUMENT_PAPER/PRA_INST.PDF) documents 1.2 kHz–40.55 MHz coverage, but a calibrated quiet-sky diffuse brightness spectrum with beam and interference selection has not been verified. Raw planetary-radio browse data do not by themselves supply such a measurement. No Voyager points are included.

For a defensible paper panel, show the tabular observations as points grouped by source and north/south, with error bars identified as published accuracy estimates, and overlay a lowsky prediction extracted through matching historical response/sky masks. A separate all-sky lowsky mean curve may illustrate scale but should be labeled as a different statistic. Show the frequency-dependent RAE beam caveat prominently below about 2 MHz.
