# Bright-source observational constraints (bounded pass, 2026-10-13)

`measurements.json` contains 35 numerical observational records: 27 published LWA1 ratios (nine bands for Cas A, Tau A, Vir A relative to Cyg A), four Cas A peak/ratio results from 2019/2023.5 experiments, three spatially resolved Cyg A spectral slopes, and one 2025 Crab shell peak. Its four additional A-team rows are **imposed calibration-model anchors**, not independent LOFAR integrated-flux observations. `source_manifest.json` lists four downloaded original arXiv HTML articles (952,138 bytes total, SHA-256 per file) and two primary publisher pages consulted without local copies. These are modest paper/table products; no survey images or large visibility sets were downloaded.

## Critical lineage and model interpretation

The `ATEAM` entries in [`src/lowsky/pipeline.py`](../../../../src/lowsky/pipeline.py) use `S50` of 27,104, 22,146, 2,008, and 2,635 Jy for Cas, Cyg, Tau, and Vir. These exactly equal Table 1 of [de Gasperin et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.10431). The paper says its LOFAR component models were rescaled to external integrated spectra, chiefly [Perley & Butler (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05940), with the Vir A scale from [de Gasperin et al. (2012)](https://arxiv.org/html/1210.1346). Table 1's 150-MHz and 1.4-GHz numbers are useful higher-frequency **model anchors** but are also imposed curves. None should be plotted as independent LOFAR total-flux detections or used to score the existing S50 choice.

`ateam_integrated_k_sr` computes `S50*(nu/50)^alpha`, then applies an internal factor with `tau10*((nu/10)^-2.1 - 5^-2.1)` clipped to nonnegative, plus modeled Milky Way attenuation. There is no source epoch/fading parameter. A comparison must establish whether the input 50-MHz calibration values already contain real foreground/internal attenuation before applying additional terms; double-counting is a **possibility to test**, not an established bug. The 2020 LOFAR paper explicitly discusses Cas A's front/back partial covering, whereas the lowsky source term uses a single full-source internal exponential. The A-team is inserted as harmonic point sources; source extraction from a real image needs beam and aperture matching.

The [LWA1 Table 2](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/469/4/4537/3815529) records are the cleanest modest numerical comparison. Images were acquired March 28 and April 30, 2013, with a May 11 RFI replacement; beam FWHM varies about 4.7° to 2.0° across 35–80 MHz. Source flux extraction subtracts a local annulus background and fits a Gaussian. Ratios avoid much absolute-scale uncertainty but inherit structured-background, low-elevation and shared Cyg A errors; the paper estimates about 20% absolute flux calibration. Source-flux and ratio errors should not be treated as independent Gaussian per frequency without covariance information.

## Proposed panels and measurement operators

1. **Low-band ratio panel, 35–80 MHz:** plot the 27 LWA1 ratios with reported errors, each object's predicted integrated flux divided by predicted Cyg A flux. Emulate the annulus and Gaussian estimator in beam-smoothed synthetic maps if possible, especially Cas A near structured Galactic emission. For a first transparent curve, show raw source-only ratio explicitly as an approximation. A ratio to Cyg A cannot validate Cyg A's absolute S50.
2. **Cas A turnover and evolution:** separate 2019 and 2023.5 spectra/fit summaries. Epoch-correct or add a time parameter before quantitatively testing one fixed lowsky curve. Show intrinsic front/back partial covering plus external foreground as an alternative physical operator. Fit-derived peak positions are correlated with the underlying spectra; count them once.
3. **Morphology / absorption:** convolve 2020 LOFAR component images and model outputs to common resolution and restrict to the same `uv` response. LOFAR discarded baselines below 30 wavelengths to remove Galactic structure, so the maps do not measure diffuse sky zero level. Quantify Cas central depression, Cyg hotspot-to-lobe ratios, Tau compact-to-shell, and Vir halo fraction, rather than calling component spectra integrated measurements. The released [LOFAR A-team models](https://www.lofar-surveys.org/ateam.html) need explicit license/data URL and format checks before ingestion.
4. **Higher-frequency anchor:** show 150 MHz and 1.4 GHz Table 1 only as calibration-model curve markers, perhaps gray open symbols. Direct independent high-frequency photometry was not acquired in this bounded pass, and the current 1–50 MHz lowsky baseline must be separately validated above 50 MHz before extrapolation.

## Acquisition gaps and limits

The LWA1 numerical Table 2 was transcribed from the publisher's rendered primary page; command-line download returned an access block, so no original HTML file/hash is claimed for that table. Its nine nominal band centers are sufficient for an exploratory ratio plot; [Table 1](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/469/4/4537/3815529) has more precise center frequencies for final modeling. The NenuFAR Crab publisher page blocked command-line retrieval; only abstract-level results are retained. Neither a 2025 Crab numerical spectrum nor raw GURT/NenuFAR spectra were retrieved. A truly independent Cyg A absolute integrated spectrum at 1–100 MHz remains an acquisition gap. Literature 26.3-MHz absolute fluxes and original sub-30-MHz Vir/Tau tables were identified but not transcribed or independently verified. No claim is made that this is an exhaustive search of work published by October 13, 2026.
